Dot height starts at 1.050 — the same plane as the land sheet below, so the continents and their dots are coplanar. The two colours are lit onto the glass itself, so the coloured edge is the globe's own edge. Nothing to size.

Sea spacing is a multiple of the dot spacing above, not its own number — one slider still owns density. Both default to parity, so the planet carries one even field and the coast seam is exact: at 1× the two passes are the same spiral split between them. The water is here so a wave doesn't die at the coast. Spacing and the checkbox rebuild the field; brightness is free. The Dots readout is land + sea.

The land sheet floats between the glass and the dots. It takes no edge light of its own — its colour comes only from the direction the orb is lit, so it can float as high as you like without drawing a second rim around the planet. Glow pickup at 0 is flat white; at 1 there is no white left in it. It shares the dot plane at 1.050 by default — the dots draw after it with depth testing off, so coplanar is safe and the two slide together rather than one under the other.

Volcanoes loading…

Flat marks, not mountains — the edifices are the next pass. Cool cyan is the quiet activity tier (128 volcanoes with ten or more eruptions since 1900), sized by how much history and how many people are behind each one. Pale gold is erupting right now, drawn whatever its history — six of the volcanoes erupting today would be missed by the tier alone. A hollow ring is underwater, where a mountain would be a lie; Ahyi is erupting 55 m down as you read this. The eruption feed is a Cloudflare Function, so on a local server it will say it is unavailable — that is the honest path working, not a bug.

Every pair except Ember stays clear of the fixed hazard colours — no Saffir-Simpson hue, no watch/warning hue, nothing sitting on the USGS shaking scale. Ember is your reference image, for comparison. These move the globe only — the map underneath stays Ultraviolet. A world owns one palette, and rebuilding the basemap on every dropdown change is the slowest thing a world switch does. If another pair is ever promoted, it becomes its own world.

At true speed a wave takes about 95 minutes to cross the planet, and out at space distance it moves a twentieth of a pixel a second. That is why the multipliers are here.

Tap the planet to put a wave where you tapped. Everything else is the app's: drag to spin, pinch or scroll to zoom, two fingers to rotate, arrows to pan, +/− to zoom, Esc to fly back out. F fires a wave at the centre; 1 and 3 switch worlds. Zoom all the way in and the real map takes over, exactly as it does in Landfall.

FPS only means something while something is moving — frames stop when the globe is at rest, which is what keeps a phone cool.
