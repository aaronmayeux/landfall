For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
A non-tropical gale-force low pressure area centered a few hundred 
miles southeast of the North Carolina coast is producing 
disorganized showers and thunderstorms primarily along its 
associated stationary front. This low is forecast to move northward 
into unfavorable environmental conditions, and development into a 
tropical or subtropical system is no longer expected.

Regardless of the system's status, this large weather system is 
still expected to produce gusty winds, dangerous surf, rip currents, 
and coastal flooding along portions of the southeast and 
mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into early next week. In addition, heavy 
rainfall and the threat of flash flooding will move northward from 
coastal portions of the Carolinas to the mid-Atlantic over the next 
day or so. Interests along the U.S. East Coast should continue to 
monitor the progress of this system. Additional information on this 
system, including Gale Warnings, can be found in High Seas Forecasts 
issued by the National Weather Service, as well as products issued 
by your local National Weather Service forecast office.
