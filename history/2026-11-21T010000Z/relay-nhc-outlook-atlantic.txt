Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
700 PM EST Fri Nov 20 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
A complex and large low pressure system includes three embedded low 
pressure centers, one over the Gulf of America, another over 
west-central Florida, and a third a few hundred miles east-northeast 
of the northwestern Bahamas. All of these systems are currently 
non-tropical. However, the system over the western Atlantic still 
has some potential to acquire subtropical or tropical 
characteristics over the next day or two while it moves northward, 
passing near the North Carolina and mid-Atlantic coasts on Sunday. 

Although the chances of subtropical or tropical development appear 
to be decreasing, this large weather system is still expected to 
produce gusty winds, dangerous surf, rip currents and coastal 
flooding along portions of the southeast and mid-Atlantic U.S. 
coasts into early next week. In addition, heavy rainfall and the 
threat of flash flooding will continue across portions of the 
northeast Florida peninsula tonight, spreading northward over 
coastal portions of the Carolinas and the mid-Atlantic over the 
weekend. Interests along the eastern coast of the United States 
should continue to monitor the progress of this system. Additional 
information on this system, including Gale Warnings, can be found in 
High Seas Forecasts issued by the National Weather Service, as well 
as products issued by your local National Weather Service forecast 
office.
