Tropical Weather Outlook
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
100 PM EST Fri Nov 20 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
Several broad areas of low pressure are located along a front that 
extends from central Florida eastward into the Atlantic for several 
hundred miles, and are producing disorganized showers and 
thunderstorms.  One of the low pressure areas, near the central 
Florida coast, is likely to remain inland without significant 
development.  A second low farther east could acquire subtropical or 
tropical characteristics while it moves generally northward to 
north-northeastward, passing near or east of the mid-Atlantic coast 
through the weekend.

Regardless of subtropical or tropical development, this large 
weather system is expected to produce gusty winds, dangerous surf, 
rip currents and coastal flooding along portions of the southeast 
and mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into early next week.  In addition, 
heavy rainfall and the threat of flash flooding will continue 
across portions of the northeast Florida peninsula today, spreading 
northward over coastal portions of the Carolinas and the 
mid-Atlantic over the weekend.  Interests along the eastern coast of 
the United States should continue to monitor the progress of this 
system.  Additional information on this system, including Gale 
Warnings, can be found in High Seas Forecasts issued by the National 
Weather Service, as well as products issued by your local National 
Weather Service forecast office.
