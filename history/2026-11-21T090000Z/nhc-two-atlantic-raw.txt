Tropical Weather Outlook

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
A complex and large low pressure system includes three embedded low 
pressure centers, one over the Gulf of America, another over 
northern Florida, and a third a few hundred miles northeast of the 
northwestern Bahamas. All of these systems are currently 
non-tropical. However, the system over the western Atlantic still 
has some potential to acquire subtropical or tropical 
characteristics over the next day or two while it moves northward, 
passing near the North Carolina and mid-Atlantic coasts on Sunday. 

Although the chances of subtropical or tropical development continue 
to decrease, this large weather system is still expected to produce 
gusty winds, dangerous surf, rip currents, and coastal flooding 
along portions of the southeast and mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into 
early next week. In addition, heavy rainfall and the threat of flash 
flooding will continue across portions of northeastern Florida 
today, spreading northward over coastal portions of the Carolinas 
and the mid-Atlantic through the weekend. Interests along the U.S. 
East Coast should continue to monitor the progress of this system. 
Additional information on this system, including Gale Warnings, can 
be found in High Seas Forecasts issued by the National Weather 
Service, as well as products issued by your local National Weather 
Service forecast office.
