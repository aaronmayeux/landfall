For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
A non-tropical gale-force low pressure area centered several 
hundred miles offshore of northeastern Florida is producing 
disorganized showers and thunderstorms primarily along a stationary 
front. This low is forecast to move northward into an environment 
that will probably not be conducive for development of significant 
tropical or subtropical characteristics. 

Although the chances of tropical development remain low, this large 
weather system is still expected to produce gusty winds, dangerous 
surf, rip currents, and coastal flooding along portions of the 
southeast and mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into early next week. In 
addition, heavy rainfall and the threat of flash flooding will move 
northward over coastal portions of the Carolinas and the 
mid-Atlantic through the weekend. Interests along the U.S. East 
Coast should continue to monitor the progress of this system. 
Additional information on this system, including Gale Warnings, can 
be found in High Seas Forecasts issued by the National Weather 
Service, as well as products issued by your local National Weather 
Service forecast office.
