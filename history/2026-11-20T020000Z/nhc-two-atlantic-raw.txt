Tropical Weather Outlook

700 PM EST Thu Nov 19 2026

For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
Disorganized showers and thunderstorms extending across portions of 
the Florida peninsula and the Bahamas are associated with a frontal 
boundary. A non-tropical area of low pressure is forecast to form 
along this boundary just east of the Florida peninsula, or over the 
northern Bahamas, on Friday. This system could acquire subtropical 
or tropical characteristics while it moves generally northward to 
north-northeastward near or just offshore of the southeastern United 
States coast through the weekend.

Regardless of tropical or subtropical development, the system is 
expected to produce strong, gusty winds, dangerous surf, rip 
currents and coastal flooding along portions of the southeast and 
mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into early next week. In addition, heavy 
rainfall and the threat of flash flooding will continue across 
portions of the Florida peninsula through Friday, potentially 
spreading northward toward coastal portions of the Carolinas and the 
mid-Atlantic over the weekend. Interests along the eastern coast of 
the United States should continue to monitor the progress of this 
system. Additional information on this system, including Gale 
Warnings, can be found in High Seas Forecasts issued by the National 
Weather Service, as well as products issued by your local National 
Weather Service forecast office.
