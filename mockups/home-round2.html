Home drawer — Gemini's five concepts, drawn against real bytes

Built from eleven real NHC Forecast/Advisories for Tropical Storm Bertha, fetched from the 2026 archive. Home is New Orleans. Nothing on this page is illustrative — every figure went through the repo's own geometry, closest-approach and cone-error code. Three of the five are changed from Gemini's spec; the reason is under each phone, and two of those changes are the difference between a real number and an invented one.

The three findings that came out of building these

1. The storm came twice as close as advisory 10 said, and the band knew. Advisory 10's headline was a 31.6 nm closest pass. The observed track passed 16.8 nm from the house at advisory 14. Every point estimate across the whole storm was roughly a factor of two too far out — and every single one of them had the truth inside its two-thirds band. That is the strongest possible argument for never shipping the figure without the band, and it is now measured rather than argued.

2. The 12-hourly points say "no tropical-storm winds at your house". They are wrong. At every published forecast hour the 34 kt edge stays clear of New Orleans — closest miss 17.4 nm. Interpolate along the track and the edge crosses the house for 2.5 hours, reaching 3.8 nm past it. Same class of error as the ring-crossing snap: sampling a boundary at 12-hour intervals misses the boundary.

3. New Orleans was on Bertha's narrow side, and that is why she nearly missed it. Her 34 kt field reached 90–100 nm southeast and 30–40 nm northwest. The house sat northwest. Had the same storm passed at the same 48 nm on its other flank, the house would have been 40 nm deep inside tropical-storm winds instead of 17 nm clear of them. A circular wind radius gets that backwards, and every one of these charts had to carry the asymmetry.
